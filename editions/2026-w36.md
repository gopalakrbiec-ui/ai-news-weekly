## Themes of the week

1. **Nvidia is buying its way around every seam in the stack it already dominates.** In the same week, Nvidia is reported as landlord and chip supplier in Anthropic's $35B Lambda deal *and* signed to acquire Hugging Face outright — the model/dataset hub that OpenAI's own agents breached in July. A single company sitting simultaneously as chip vendor, landlord and (pending) platform owner concentrates dependency risk across nearly every lab named in this edition.
2. **States are legislating faster than labs are slowing down.** California signed catastrophic-risk AI safeguards (SB 813/AB 1405) on Sep 9 with no federal AI statute in sight, while five major labs shipped new models in the same ten days (Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, xAI) — regulation and release cadence are moving on visibly disconnected clocks.
3. **This week's "launches" were mostly price cuts wearing a version number.** Anthropic cut cache-read pricing 75%, Meta introduced a 12–21x cheaper data-sharing tier, and Google held its introductory Flash pricing through year-end — each release led with cost compression rather than a headline capability claim, suggesting margin pressure is now the more urgent story than benchmark scores.
4. **Big-number deals are being reported well ahead of being confirmed.** The Anthropic-Lambda $35B deal ran across multiple outlets sourced only to "people familiar with the matter," and Nvidia's $12.93B Hugging Face bid is a signed agreement, not a closed one (expected H1 2027, pending regulatory approval). Read the size of this week's numbers with that caveat attached.
